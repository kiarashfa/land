Kia's *Perceptense* is a collection of bite-sized interactive modules that build an intuition about everything. The SaleRoom should share that spirit: short rounds, immediate feedback, learning by playing.

## The project, as understood so far

- **What:** a game set in a luxurious auction saleroom. Items come up as lots. The player judges their value: guess the hammer price, pick the more valuable of two, rank a set, or bid against the room. The game then reveals the real value and explains **why**.
- **Teaching goal:** what makes things valuable. Provenance, rarity, condition, craftsmanship, demand, timing, condition reports, estimates and buyer's premium, and the difference between price and value.
- **Tone:** the elegance of a great auction house. Never a game-show cartoon. The auctioneer's patter is the teacher's voice.
- **Look:** the existing saleroom, raised to realism. Real people in the room, a credible auctioneer, real items photographed or modelled well.

## Open questions for Kia (ask early, multiple choice where possible)

1. **Which items?** Fine art, watches, cars, wine, design objects, everyday things ("which costs more: a Michelin-star dinner or a vintage Rolex?"), or Kia's own categories?
2. **Real prices from real auctions,** or curated estimates? This decides the data sourcing and licensing work.
3. **Which game modes first?** See `04-game-design.md`.
4. **Solo only, or play with friends** (same room, pass the phone, or online)?
5. **Accounts, streaks, leaderboards?** (No trackers, as in Kia's other projects. Local storage only?)
6. **Sound:** the gavel, the room's murmur, the auctioneer's voice (text-to-speech or recorded)? Off by default?
7. **Phone first?** Kia's other projects are shared in person, on phones.
8. **Stack and hosting:** GitHub Pages like Kia's other projects? Astro + three.js?

## Constraints and cautions

- **Image rights.** Auction houses' photographs are copyrighted. Prefer public-domain or CC images (museum open-access collections, Wikimedia Commons), your own 3D models, or your own photography.
- **Price data.** Results from Christie's, Sotheby's and others are public facts, but their databases have terms of use. Record a source for every price.
- **No impersonation.** Don't use real auction-house names, logos or branding. The saleroom is fictional.
